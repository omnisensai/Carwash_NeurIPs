\section{Results}

% ============================================================

\subsection{Behavioural Results}
\label{sec:behavioural-results}

We measured 24 distinct models: seventeen sampled through hosted APIs and nine
open-weight models sampled locally in bf16 with a per-sample seed recorded. Two
models appear in both and are counted once, from the local rows. Every model
receives byte-identical prompt text, $R=10$ samples per condition. One model
(Qwen2.5-3B) is reproducibly correct at baseline and is excluded as ineligible for
rescue, leaving 23 models and 230 executions per condition.

\paragraph{Baseline operational state.}
Sixteen of the 23 models are reproducibly incorrect, naming the wrong action on all
ten samples. Seven are non-reproducible. Thirty-four of 230 executions name the
intended action.

\paragraph{Effects of conventional prompt interventions.}
Seven conventional interventions were applied to the same question under the same
protocol: expert role assignment, objective emphasis, chain-of-thought elicitation,
anti-hallucination instruction, threat, encouragement, and error-avoidance
instruction (Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). Expert role assignment converts one of the
sixteen reproducibly incorrect models to reproducible correctness; no conventional
condition converts more than one, and across all seven the converted model is the
same one. Four of the seven leave at least fifteen of the sixteen reproducibly
incorrect. Five of the seven move a non-reproducible model to reproducible
incorrectness: one each under anti-hallucination, chain-of-thought, error avoidance
and threat, and two under encouragement.

\paragraph{Substrate-conditioned operational state.}
Under the substrate, supplied as a system message with the question unchanged,
seventeen of the 23 models are reproducibly correct, four are non-reproducible, and
two are reproducibly incorrect (Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B-2507). One hundred and
ninety-three of 230 executions name the intended action. No model moves to
reproducible incorrectness.

\paragraph{Transitions between reproducibly incorrect, non-reproducible, and
reproducibly correct states.}
Transitions are reported separately for the two baseline failure modes

reproducible correctness, seventeen of 23 in total, in a single pass with the
question unchanged and no sampling, voting or retry. Every conventional intervention
reaches reproducible correctness in at most one model from either group.

\subsection{Internal Causal Results}
\label{sec:internal-results}

The behavioural measurement records which action a model emits. It does not say
whether the specification acts on the computation that selects the action, or
whether its effect is carried by the presence of its tokens in context. We measure
this on ten open-weight models, on prompt text byte-identical to the behavioural
runs. The readout is the decision margin
\begin{equation}
M \;=\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{D}} P(t) \;-\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{W}} P(t)
\end{equation}
in nats, at the position that predicts the first answer token, teacher-forced, under
the chat template, in bf16; $M > 0$ favours the intended action.

\paragraph{Untreated and specification-conditioned decision preference.}
Six of the ten models prefer the incorrect action untreated, with margins from
$-0.50$ to $-22.00$ nats (Table~\ref{tab:margins}). These six are the models that
can exhibit a reversal; the remaining four already prefer the intended action and
are reported for completeness only. Under the specification, four of the six cross
zero.

The conventional interventions do not. On Llama-3.3-70B, whose untreated margin is
$-13.65$, the seven conventional conditions span $-18.21$ to $-4.67$ nats: every one
remains on the incorrect side, and chain-of-thought moves the margin further from
the intended action than no intervention at all. The specification reaches $+15.01$.
On Llama-3.1-8B the pattern repeats at smaller scale: conventional conditions span
$-4.57$ to $-0.35$ against an untreated $-3.71$, and the specification reaches
$+1.51$. Across both models no conventional intervention crosses zero.

\paragraph{Activation transplantation.}
To separate the specification's effect from the presence of its tokens, we run the
untreated prompt and replace the residual stream at the answer position, at a single
layer, with the residual from the specification run at the same position and layer.
No specification token is present in the patched forward pass. In every model whose
preference the specification reverses, this transplant alone reverses the untreated
preference. The effect is therefore present as a state at one position, and is
transferable without the text that produced it.

Two controls bound the procedure. Patching a run with its own residual produces
$\Delta M = 0.00$ in all 96 cells per model, so the intervention machinery
contributes nothing on its own. Patching a random direction of matched norm produces
a maximum $|\Delta M|$ of $0.87$, $0.39$ and $2.00$ nats on Llama-3.3-70B,
Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B, against measured single-line effects reaching $2.29$,
$1.99$ and $14.75$ nats in the same models. The effect is not a norm perturbation.

A third control isolates the contribution of the specification's form. Removing
every constraint line and retaining only its section headers leaves the margin at or
below untreated in all three models the specification reverses: $-4.07$ against an
untreated $-3.71$ on Llama-3.1-8B, $-14.92$ against $-13.65$ on Llama-3.3-70B, and
$-13.75$ against $-14.06$ on Qwen3-8B. The structured format contributes nothing;
the constraint content does.

\paragraph{Layerwise causal effects.}
The transplant is effective from the middle of the stack: layer 13 of 28, 15 of 32,
38 of 80 and 24 of 36, that is between $0.48$ and $0.69$ of depth. Below those
layers the patched margin is indistinguishable from untreated; above them the
patched run selects the intended action. The decision is therefore determined well
before the layers at which the unembedding makes it visible --- the logit lens
resolves the reversal only in the final layers, underestimating by tens of layers
the depth at which the constraint is installed.

The three Llama models cross at $0.48$ of depth in each case, across a
$2.9\times$ range in layer count, and do so as a step: the patched margin is flat at
the untreated value and then moves to its plateau within one tenth of depth. Qwen3-8B
crosses at $0.69$ and climbs gradually across the final third. With one crossing
Qwen in the set we report the difference in depth and shape without attributing it
to the family.

\paragraph{Successful and unsuccessful preference reversals.}
Four of the six models that prefer the incorrect action untreated cross zero under
the specification; two do not. Both failures move substantially toward the intended
action without arriving: Qwen2.5-7B from $-10.50$ to $-1.50$, a displacement of
$9.0$ nats, and Qwen3-4B-2507 from $-22.00$ to $-5.50$, a displacement of $16.5$
nats. Their layerwise curves have the same shape as the successful cases, rising
monotonically across the back half of the stack, and terminate short of zero at the
final layer. Their behavioural rows agree: both emit the incorrect action on all ten
samples under the specification.

These are not cases in which the specification fails to act. They are cases in which
it acts and the displacement is insufficient. The distinction bears on what the
method claims: a specification that reverses four preferences of six, and moves the
margin by $9$ to $16$ nats in the two it does not reverse, is an intervention of
graded strength rather than a switch.

\paragraph{Relationship between internal and behavioural measurements.}
The two measurements are taken on the same models and the same prompt text by
different procedures --- one deterministic forward pass against ten sampled
generations --- and they agree quantitatively. Treating $\sigma(M)$ as a predicted
count of intended actions in ten samples, predicted and observed counts agree within
$1.5$ samples in 14 of the 18 model--condition cells measured both ways, with a
maximum discrepancy of $2.2$ samples. In no cell do the two disagree in sign: every
model's majority sampled action matches the sign of its margin.

The four larger discrepancies are all in the direction of greater determinism than
the margin predicts: models at intermediate margins emit the favoured action more
consistently than a first-token reading implies. We record this as a property of the
relationship between the two readouts rather than a correction to either, since the
margin is measured at a single teacher-forced position while sampling runs the full
generation.
