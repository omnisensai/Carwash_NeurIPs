% ============================================================
\section{Internal Measurement}
\label{sec:internal-measurement}
% ============================================================

This section reports internal measurements on the ten open-weight models of the
fleet. They are reported because they bear on the reproducibility claim: they
establish that the behavioural transition of Section~\ref{sec:results} corresponds to
a change in the computation, and that the state carrying it can be transplanted. They
are not a mechanistic account, and none is claimed.

\subsection{Measurement design}

\paragraph{The standard instrument, and what we change about it.}
Causal analysis of language models transplants hidden states between two runs of the
same model and measures the effect on the output
\citep{vig2020causal,meng2022locating}, commonly read out as a logit difference
between two candidate answer tokens \citep{wang2023interpretability}; we follow the
methodological recommendations of \citet{zhang2024towards}. The standard contrast is a
clean prompt against a minimally corrupted one --- one token replaced, or noise added
to one span --- and the outcome variable is the internal quantity itself. Two
properties of that protocol do not transfer to the question asked here.

First, the outcome variable. A logit difference can move substantially while the
emitted token does not change: in this fleet, the seven conventional interventions
shift the margin by up to $2.2$ nats on Llama-3.1-8B without altering the emitted
word. Taking the internal quantity as the outcome would count those as effects. We
take the outcome to be a change in the emitted action, and admit a model--condition to
the causal analysis only when the specification changes it.

Second, the reference. Comparing internal states across models requires that the
models be doing the same thing, and on an untreated task they are not: which models
appear anomalous depends on the task chosen and on which models were tested, and the
difference between a model that selects the intended action on three of ten samples
and one that selects it on none has no fixed standard against which either is
measured. The behavioural convergence established in Section~\ref{sec:results}
supplies that standard. It is verified per model--condition before any activation is
inspected, and it holds the input, the emitted action and the correctness criterion
fixed across the compared models by construction.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Ten instruction-tuned models spanning $0.5$B to $70$B parameters and $24$ to $80$
layers are run in \texttt{bf16} with each model's own chat template. For each prompt
we take a single forward pass and read, at the first answer token,
\[
M \;=\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{D}} P(t) \;-\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{W}} P(t),
\]
where $\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{W}$ are the measured surface-form token families for
the two actions (Section~\ref{sec:measurement}). $M$ is a log-ratio between two token
families and is reported as such; in three of the $90$ cells of
Table~\ref{tab:internal-margins} its sign and the greedy token disagree, and we report
the disagreement rather than resolving it in favour of the margin.

For the causal readout we run the specification prompt, capture its residual stream at
the answer position after each layer $\ell$, and write that vector into the
corresponding position of the untreated forward pass, leaving every other position
untouched. The remaining $L-\ell$ layers of the untreated computation then run
normally over the patched state. Because our two conditions differ by the whole
specification rather than by one token, no positional attribution follows, and we
sweep depth at the answer position rather than the layer $\times$ position grid. The
experiment resolves depth, not sequence position.

\paragraph{Controls.}
Patching a run into itself changes the margin by exactly zero at every layer.
Replacing the transplanted vector with a random direction of the same norm as the
difference between conditions moves the margin by $1.8$ nats on Llama-3.3-70B and
$2.75$ on Qwen3-8B, against effects of $9.5$ and $7.75$ in the same runs; on
Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-3.2-3B it reaches $0.50$ and $0.34$ against effects of $0.74$
and $-0.48$, so those two models are at their own noise floor at line resolution and
no line-level conclusion is drawn from them. A control question whose correct action
is \texttt{walk} remains \texttt{walk} under every patch on every model.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
The internal measurements were run on an earlier specification
(\texttt{sha256:6f89be2b}) than the behavioural sweep (\texttt{sha256:5b56feb3}). The
untreated prompt is identical across both (\texttt{sha256:f9ac23fb}), as are the
conventional intervention prompts. This section therefore reports a related
specification condition, not the internal counterpart of the $163/170$ result.

\paragraph{The incorrect action can carry nearly all of the measured probability.}
Six of the ten models prefer the incorrect action untreated. On four of them the
\textsc{walk} family carries essentially the entire measured distribution:
$P(\mathcal{W}) = 1.000$ to three decimals on Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-4B-2507
and Qwen3-8B, and $0.975$ on Llama-3.1-8B. Llama-3.2-3B is the one near-indifferent
case, at $0.606$ against $0.375$.

\paragraph{Conventional interventions move the margin, often away from the correct action.}
Across ten models and seven conventional conditions, $27$ of $70$ leave the margin
further from the correct action than the untreated task does. On Llama-3.2-3B all
seven do. On Llama-3.1-8B, error avoidance, encouragement and threat take a model at
$-3.69$ to $-4.74$, $-5.87$ and $-5.74$. The behavioural sweep records these
conditions as unchanged, because the emitted word is unchanged. Of the six models that
prefer the incorrect action untreated, one conventional condition on one model
produces a positive margin: objective emphasis on Qwen3-4B-2507, at $+6.25$. On the
remaining five the best conventional margin is $1.00$ to $5.25$ nats short of zero.

\paragraph{The specification does not name the action it produces.}
Neither specification contains the word \texttt{drive}. Both name \emph{walking} ---
the incorrect action --- as the operation that fails to satisfy the objective. Both
actions appear in the question itself, identically in every condition. The
specification determines the answer, as any constraint set is meant to; it does not
supply it.

\paragraph{Transplantation reverses the emitted action on three models.}
On Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen3-8B --- the three models where the
specification changes the emitted action, and therefore the three admitted to the
causal analysis --- writing the specification-conditioned residual into the untreated
forward pass at a sufficiently deep layer moves the final margin from $-3.69$,
$-13.78$ and $-14.40$ to $+1.01$, $+6.07$ and $+10.75$, in each case equal to the
specification prompt's own margin to two decimals. Two further models are reported for
completeness and excluded from the analysis: on Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B-2507 the
specification does not change the emitted action, and no transplantation layer does
either. Llama-3.2-3B is also excluded: the specification leaves it at $-0.01$, so
there is no behavioural transition to study, and its transplantation result of $+0.47$
is a change in a patched computation with no corresponding prompt-level state.

\paragraph{The layer at which transplantation becomes sufficient is consistent across scale.}
Transplanting at early layers leaves the final margin unchanged. On Llama-3.1-8B the
patched margin sits at $\approx-3.58$ for layers $0$ through $12$, then moves through
$-3.07$, $-1.72$ and $-0.11$ at layers $13$ to $15$, becomes positive at layer $16$,
and does not increase further. The first positive layer is $16/32$ on Llama-3.1-8B and
$41/80$ on Llama-3.3-70B --- relative depths of $0.50$ and $0.51$ across a $9\times$
range in parameter count --- and $23/36$, or $0.64$, on Qwen3-8B. This is a property
of the transplantation experiment and not a localisation: a patch at layer $8$ leaves
$24$ subsequent layers of untreated computation to act on the state, and its failure
to change the final margin does not establish that the relevant information is absent
there. What the sweep measures is the depth at which the specification-conditioned
state becomes sufficient to reverse the final preference under the remaining
computation. Over the same layers, cosine similarity between the untreated and
specification-conditioned residuals at this position on Llama-3.1-8B falls from
$0.96$--$0.98$ through layer $14$ to $0.91$ at layer $16$, reaching its minimum of
$0.90$ at layers $18$--$19$.

\paragraph{Behavioural agreement does not imply that the same content carries the decision.}
A position-resolved variant of the experiment replaces one line of the specification
at a time with a rewrite of identical token count that neutralises or reverses its
semantics. Two results follow. Reversing the objective line --- from
\texttt{while transporting the object from location A to B} to
\texttt{without transporting the object from location A to B} --- does not weaken the
correct preference on Llama-3.3-70B; it strengthens it, from $+6.07$ to $+9.04$. And
the constraint that carries the effect is not a property of the specification:
reversing line~5 moves Llama-3.3-70B by $9.51$ nats and reverses its decision, while
on Qwen3-8B the same rewrite of the same line moves the margin below the measurement
resolution of that run. Both models reach the same reproducible action from the same
specification. Behavioural convergence therefore does not entail that the same
specification content is doing the work inside them, and neither the direction of a
constraint's contribution nor its identity can be read off the text.

\subsection{Scope}

These measurements establish that the specification changes the models' internal
output preference, and that transplanting the specification-conditioned state into the
untreated computation can reverse its emitted action. They do not establish how
individual constraints are represented, which components write them, where in the
network any decision is formed, or whether the effect transfers to other tasks. The
line-level results identify constraints the measured effect depends on under a
declared counterfactual; deletion and reversal disagree --- deleting line~6 reverses
Llama-3.1-8B's decision while rewriting it to the opposite does not --- so which line
appears to carry the effect depends on which intervention is used. Establishing the
dependence structure itself requires ablations across many specifications and models,
together with attribution and pathway analysis, and is the subject of separate work.
