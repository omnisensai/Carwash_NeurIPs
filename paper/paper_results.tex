\subsection{Sampled Results}
\label{sec:behavioural-results}

The question is whether a model that fails this decision can be moved to
reproducible correctness. We measured 28 models that fail it at baseline, either
confidently wrong or unreliable: each names the wrong action on all ten baseline
samples, or names different actions across them. Fourteen are sampled
through hosted APIs and fourteen are open-weight models sampled locally in bf16 with
a per-sample seed recorded. Every model receives byte-identical prompt text, $R=10$
samples per condition, giving 280 executions per condition. The baseline prompt,
the control, the seven conventional prompts and the substrate are reproduced
verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}, each with the sha256 digest recorded with
every execution.

\paragraph{Baseline operational state.}
Twenty-one of the 28 models are reproducibly incorrect, naming the wrong action on
all ten samples. Seven are non-reproducible. Thirty-four of 280 executions name the
intended action.

\paragraph{Control: the answer stated in the prompt.}
A control condition states the intended action outright --- ``The correct answer is
drive.'' --- inserted where the conventional lines go. It is a control rather than a
prompting technique: an operator who already knows the answer has no decision to
delegate. Eighteen of the 28 models are reproducibly correct under it, three are
non-reproducible and seven are reproducibly incorrect; 197 of 280 executions name the
intended action. Thirteen of the twenty-one models reproducibly incorrect at baseline
and five of the seven non-reproducible ones reach reproducible correctness. Seven
remain reproducibly incorrect, naming the wrong action on all ten samples while the
prompt states the right one: Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Claude Sonnet 4.6,
GPT-4.1, GPT-4o, Llama 3.3-70B and Mistral Large. All seven are reproducibly correct
under the substrate, whose text does not contain the word naming the intended
action.

\paragraph{Effects of conventional prompt interventions.}
Seven conventional interventions were applied to the same question under the same
protocol: expert role assignment, objective emphasis, chain-of-thought elicitation,
anti-hallucination instruction, threat, encouragement, and error-avoidance
instruction. Each adds a single line to the baseline prompt, inserted between the
question and the answer instruction, except expert role assignment, which precedes
the question; the texts are reproduced verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} gives the operational state of the 28 models under every
condition, abbreviated RC for reproducibly correct, NR for non-reproducible and RI
for reproducibly incorrect. Expert role assignment converts one of the
twenty-one reproducibly incorrect models to reproducible correctness; no conventional
condition converts more than one, and across all seven the converted model is the
same one. Four of the seven leave at least twenty of the twenty-one reproducibly
incorrect. Five of the seven move a non-reproducible model to reproducible
incorrectness: one each under anti-hallucination, chain-of-thought, error avoidance
and threat, and two under encouragement.

\paragraph{Substrate-conditioned operational state.}
Under the substrate, supplied as a system message with the question unchanged,
twenty-two of the 28 models are reproducibly correct, four are non-reproducible, and
two are reproducibly incorrect (Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B-2507);
Table~\ref{tab:per-model} gives every model's count at all three conditions. Two
hundred and forty-three of 280 executions name the intended action. Eighteen of the
twenty-one models that are reproducibly incorrect at baseline and four of the seven
that are non-reproducible reach reproducible correctness, in a single pass with the
question unchanged and no sampling, voting or retry. No model moves to reproducible
incorrectness. One model names the intended action less often than at baseline
(Qwen2.5-0.5B, 7/10 to 3/10) without leaving the non-reproducible state, and one is
unchanged sample for sample (Llama 3.2-3B, 4/10 at both conditions, the same four
sample indices).

The substrate and the control are not nested. Thirteen models are reproducibly correct
under both, five under the control alone, nine under the substrate alone, and one
(Qwen2.5-0.5B) under neither; the union is 27 of 28. The two models the substrate
leaves reproducibly incorrect are both reproducibly correct under the control, so
neither is unable to name the intended action on this task.
