\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Behavioural Results}
\label{sec:behavioural-results}

The question is whether a model that fails this decision can be moved to
reproducible correctness. We measured 23 models that fail it at baseline, either
confidently wrong or unreliable: each names the wrong action on all ten baseline
samples, or names different actions across them. Fifteen are sampled
through hosted APIs and eight are open-weight models sampled locally in bf16 with a
per-sample seed recorded. Every model receives byte-identical prompt text, $R=10$
samples per condition, giving 230 executions per condition. The baseline prompt,
the seven conventional prompts and the substrate are reproduced verbatim in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}, each with the sha256 digest recorded with every
execution.

\paragraph{Baseline operational state.}
Sixteen of the 23 models are reproducibly incorrect, naming the wrong action on all
ten samples. Seven are non-reproducible. Thirty-four of 230 executions name the
intended action.

\paragraph{Effects of conventional prompt interventions.}
Seven conventional interventions were applied to the same question under the same
protocol: expert role assignment, objective emphasis, chain-of-thought elicitation,
anti-hallucination instruction, threat, encouragement, and error-avoidance
instruction. Each adds a single line to the baseline prompt, inserted between the
question and the answer instruction, except expert role assignment, which precedes
the question; the texts are reproduced verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} gives the operational state of the 23 models under every
condition, abbreviated RC for reproducibly correct, NR for non-reproducible and RI
for reproducibly incorrect. Expert role assignment converts one of the
sixteen reproducibly incorrect models to reproducible correctness; no conventional
condition converts more than one, and across all seven the converted model is the
same one. Four of the seven leave at least fifteen of the sixteen reproducibly
incorrect. Five of the seven move a non-reproducible model to reproducible
incorrectness: one each under anti-hallucination, chain-of-thought, error avoidance
and threat, and two under encouragement.

\paragraph{Substrate-conditioned operational state.}
Under the substrate, supplied as a system message with the question unchanged,
seventeen of the 23 models are reproducibly correct, four are non-reproducible, and
two are reproducibly incorrect (Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B-2507);
Table~\ref{tab:per-model} gives every model's count at both conditions. One hundred and
ninety-three of 230 executions name the intended action. Thirteen of the sixteen
models that are reproducibly incorrect at baseline and four of the seven that are
non-reproducible reach reproducible correctness, in a single pass with the question
unchanged and no sampling, voting or retry. No model moves to reproducible
incorrectness. One model reproducibly incorrect at baseline reaches the intended
action on most but not all samples (DeepSeek V3.2, 0/10 to 8/10); it names the
incorrect action on all ten samples at baseline and under all seven conventional
conditions. One model names the intended action less often than at baseline
(Qwen2.5-0.5B, 7/10 to 3/10) without leaving the non-reproducible state, and one is
unchanged sample for sample (Llama 3.2-3B, 4/10 at both conditions, the same four
sample indices).

\subsection{The Decision Margin}
\label{sec:internal-results}

Nine of the 23 models were run locally in bf16 on the same prompt files as the
behavioural runs. The readout is the decision margin
\begin{equation}
M \;=\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{D}} P(t) \;-\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{W}} P(t)
\end{equation}
in nats at the \emph{answer position} --- the final token of the prompt once the
chat template has been applied --- teacher-forced, with no generation, so what
is measured is the distribution the model would sample from rather than a
sampled outcome. $M>0$ favours the intended action.

Table~\ref{tab:margins} gives the result. Two things follow from it. The
$0/10 \to 10/10$ transitions of Section~\ref{sec:behavioural-results} correspond
to shifts of $5$ to $29$ nats, so they are changes in the underlying
distribution and not favourable draws at temperature 1.0. And the nine models
divide at four billion parameters: the five at or above it are confidently
wrong at baseline ($-3.69$ to $-21.75$) and the substrate shifts every one by at
least $5.07$ nats, while the four below sit within $2.25$ nats of indifference
and shift by at most $2.37$. The intervention needs a model that holds a
position in order to move it off one. This bounds the claim rather than
weakening it: a specification that made a half-billion-parameter model reliable
would be a claim about replacing scale, which is not what we assert.

\paragraph{What margin reproducible correctness requires.}
The margin and the sampled count are linked by the number of samples the
protocol demands. Writing $p = \sigma(M)$ for the probability that a single
draw names the intended action, reproducible correctness over $R$ samples has
probability $p^{R}$, so the margin needed grows with $R$: at $R=10$, an even
chance of $10/10$ needs $M \ge +2.63$ nats, a $90\%$ chance needs $+4.55$, and
$99\%$ needs $+6.90$. Crossing zero is not close --- at $M=0$ the chance of
$10/10$ is $0.1\%$. Three of the nine models reach $+2.63$ under the substrate.
This gives a target an intervention must hit rather than merely a direction it
must move, and it is the sense in which we report reproducibility rather than
accuracy: a mean over samples is passed by margins that $10/10$ rejects.

The relation is an approximation in two ways. It treats the intended and
unintended actions as exhausting the distribution, and it assumes draws are
independent. It holds across these models --- predicted and observed counts
agree within $1.4$ samples in eight of the nine --- with one exception, Llama
3.1-8B, whose substrate margin of $+1.39$ implies a $10.8\%$ chance of $10/10$
and which returned $10/10$. That is an unlikely draw rather than an impossible
one, but with one cell we cannot separate chance from a failure of the
approximation, and a framework built on this relation should be calibrated on
more than nine models before it is relied on.

A positive margin is not a correct answer. The three models below the rule
favour the intended action at the answer position while remaining
non-reproducible when sampled --- at $M=+0.50$ the model prefers it in roughly
$62\%$ of draws, and Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen3-0.6B return $7/10$,
$6/10$ and $7/10$ at baseline. They are part of the 23-model population, which
excludes only models reproducibly correct at baseline.

\subsection{Activation Transplantation}
\label{sec:transplant}

A margin shift shows the substrate changes what the model is disposed to
answer. It does not show the change is carried by the substrate's own state
rather than by receiving some framing other than the baseline. To separate
those, we transplant the state itself: we run the \emph{baseline} prompt and
replace the residual stream at the answer position, after a single layer, with
the residual of a donor condition at the same position and layer, then read $M$
from the model's output. No donor text is present in the patched pass.

With the substrate as donor, the transplant alone reverses the baseline
preference on all three reversal models that fit the measurement: after layer 15
of 32 on Llama 3.1-8B, 24 of 36 on Qwen3-8B, 38 of 80 on Llama 3.3-70B.
Figure~\ref{fig:patching} repeats it with each of the seven conventional
conditions as donor. None reverses the preference at any layer. The donors are
not inert --- they move the margin by up to $58\%$ of the substrate's
displacement --- but the movement does not carry the decision, and its direction
depends on the donor: on Qwen3-8B five of the seven move the margin
\emph{further} from the intended action, chain-of-thought by $4.3$ nats.
Overwriting the residual at this position therefore produces no generic drift
toward the intended action. Patching a run with its own residual gives
$\Delta M = 0.0000$ at every layer of every model.

\paragraph{What Sections~\ref{sec:internal-results} and~\ref{sec:transplant} do not establish.}
These measurements locate the substrate's effect and rule out one alternative
explanation for it. They do not identify a mechanism: we do not establish what
the transplanted state encodes, which part of the substrate produces it, or why
a specification produces it where a conventional framing does not. Nor is the
transition depth a property of the substrate --- patching the control reaches
its halfway point at the same layer in four of the five models where the
comparison is defined (not plotted), so that depth is where these models settle
the decision. Llama 3.2-3B is reported but excluded from the causal claim: its
decision spans $0.55$ nats, too little to distinguish a transplant from any
perturbation large enough to tip it. Finally, the nine models here include none
of the six on which the control fails behaviourally, so they cannot speak to
those.
