% ============================================================
\section{Internal Measurement}
\label{sec:internal-measurement}
% ============================================================

The behavioural sweep records which word a model emits. This section records
what the model's residual stream carries at the position where that word is
produced, on ten open-weight models for which the activations are available.
We report decision margins for the untreated task, the conventional
interventions and the substrate, and one causal readout, activation
transplantation. Line ablations, direct logit attribution and attention
analyses are outside the scope of this paper.

\paragraph{Substrate provenance.}
The internal measurements were run on an earlier substrate specification
(\texttt{sha256:6f89be2b}) than the one used for the behavioural sweep
(\texttt{sha256:5b56feb3}). The untreated task prompt is identical across both
experiments (\texttt{sha256:f9ac23fb}), as are the conventional intervention
prompts. Section~\ref{sec:internal-measurement} therefore reports a related but
distinct substrate condition, and its margins should not be read as internal
measurements of the specification responsible for the $163/170$ behavioural
result in Section~\ref{sec:results}. Both specifications are in the repository.

\subsection{Internal Causal Measurement}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Ten instruction-tuned open-weight models spanning $0.5$B to $70$B parameters and
$24$ to $80$ layers are run in \texttt{bf16} with each model's own chat template.
For each prompt we take a single forward pass and read the decision margin at the
first answer token,
\[
M \;=\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{D}} P(t) \;-\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{W}} P(t),
\]
where $\mathcal{D}$ and $\mathcal{W}$ are the measured surface-form token families
for the two actions, as defined in Section~\ref{sec:measurement}. $M > 0$ means the
\textsc{drive} family carries more total probability than the \textsc{walk} family.
It does not guarantee that the model emits a \textsc{drive} token: in three of the
$90$ cells of Table~\ref{tab:internal-margins} the sign of $M$ and the greedy token
disagree. The internal margin is supplementary evidence about output preference,
not a substitute for the behavioural classification of Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Transplantation.}
The causal readout is activation patching, the standard causal-mediation instrument
for language models \citep{vig2020causal,meng2022locating}, read out as a logit
difference between two candidate answer tokens \citep{wang2023interpretability};
we follow the methodological recommendations of \citet{zhang2024towards}. We run the
substrate prompt, capture its residual stream at the answer position after each layer
$\ell$, and write that vector into the corresponding position of the \emph{untreated}
forward pass, leaving every other position untouched. The remaining $L - \ell$ layers
of the untreated computation then run normally over the patched state. We sweep
$\ell$ over all layers and record the resulting final margin.

Causal tracing normally contrasts a clean prompt with a minimally corrupted one ---
a single token replaced, or noise added to one span --- so that a restored answer
localises to the damaged position. The contrast used here is the whole specification
against its absence, which is the behaviourally meaningful comparison but not a
minimal one, and we sweep depth at the answer position rather than the full layer
$\times$ position grid. This section therefore resolves depth and not sequence
position: it establishes at which layer the substrate-conditioned state becomes
sufficient, and not which tokens of the specification carry the effect. A
position-resolved variant of the experiment, using token-aligned single-line
counterfactuals, was run on a subset of these models; we draw only its controls here
and reserve its maps for separate work.

\paragraph{Controls.}
Three checks accompany the transplantation. Patching a run into itself changes the
margin by exactly zero at every layer. Replacing the transplanted vector with a
random direction of the same norm as the difference between the two conditions moves
the margin by $1.8$ nats on Llama-3.3-70B and $2.75$ on Qwen3-8B, against line-level
effects of $9.5$ and $7.75$ in the same runs; on Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-3.2-3B the
random-direction control reaches $0.50$ and $0.34$ against effects of $0.74$ and
$-0.48$, so at line resolution those two models are at their own noise floor and we
do not draw line-level conclusions from them. A control question whose correct action
is \texttt{walk} remains \texttt{walk} under every patch on every model.

\paragraph{The incorrect action can carry nearly all of the measured probability.}
Six of the ten models prefer the incorrect action on the untreated task. On four of
them the \textsc{walk} family carries essentially the entire measured distribution:
$P(\mathcal{W}) = 1.000$ to three decimals on Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-7B,
Qwen3-4B-2507 and Qwen3-8B, and $0.975$ on Llama-3.1-8B. Llama-3.2-3B is the one
near-indifferent case, at $P(\mathcal{W}) = 0.606$ against
$P(\mathcal{D}) = 0.375$. The failure that the behavioural sweep records as a single
wrong word is, on five of the six models, an output distribution that places almost
no mass on the correct action.

\paragraph{Conventional interventions move the margin, often away from the correct action.}
Across all ten models and seven conventional conditions, $27$ of $70$ conditions
leave the margin further from the correct action than the untreated task does. On
Llama-3.2-3B all seven do: the margin runs from $-0.48$ untreated to as far as
$-3.25$ under encouragement. On Llama-3.1-8B, error avoidance, encouragement and
threat take a model at $-3.69$ to $-4.74$, $-5.87$ and $-5.74$. The behavioural
sweep records these conditions as unchanged, because the emitted word is unchanged.

\paragraph{One conventional condition reverses the preference.}
Of the six models that prefer the incorrect action untreated, one conventional
condition on one model produces a positive margin: objective emphasis on
Qwen3-4B-2507, at $+6.25$. On the remaining five the best conventional margin is
$1.00$ to $5.25$ nats short of zero. This is consistent with the behavioural
result, in which conventional interventions repair at most one model in the fleet.

\paragraph{The substrate does not name the action it produces.}
Neither substrate specification contains the word \texttt{drive}. Both name
\emph{walking} --- the incorrect action --- as the operation that fails to satisfy
the objective: the nine-line specification does so twice, in
\texttt{Walking leaves a vehicle behind} and
\texttt{Walking does not transport a vehicle}. Both actions appear in the question
itself, identically in every condition. The specification determines the answer, as
any constraint set is meant to; it does not supply it.

\subsection{Internal Effects of Engineered Behavioral Change}

\paragraph{Transplantation reverses the final preference on four models.}
On Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen3-8B, writing the substrate-conditioned
residual into the untreated forward pass at a sufficiently deep layer moves the
final margin from $-3.69$, $-13.78$ and $-14.40$ to $+1.01$, $+6.07$ and $+10.75$.
In each of these three the transplanted margin equals the substrate prompt's own
margin to two decimals. Llama-3.2-3B differs: the substrate prompt leaves it at
$-0.01$, while transplantation reaches $+0.47$, so the transplanted state is not
simply reproducing the substrate condition's output preference.

\paragraph{Two substrate conditions do not transfer.}
On Qwen2.5-7B the substrate condition itself yields $-3.62$ and no transplantation
layer improves on that. On Qwen3-4B-2507 the substrate condition yields $-9.75$ and
the best transplantation layer reaches $-8.75$. In both cases the substrate
specification used here does not reverse the model's preference by prompting, and
transplanting its state does not either. We report these individually rather than as
a general rule.

\paragraph{The layer at which transplantation becomes sufficient is consistent across scale.}
Transplanting at early layers leaves the final margin unchanged. On Llama-3.1-8B the
patched margin sits at $\approx -3.58$ for layers $0$ through $12$ --- within noise of
the untouched value --- then moves through $-3.07$, $-1.72$ and $-0.11$ at layers
$13$ to $15$, becomes positive at layer $16$, and does not increase further. The
layer at which the patched margin first turns positive is $14/28$ on Llama-3.2-3B,
$16/32$ on Llama-3.1-8B and $41/80$ on Llama-3.3-70B: relative depths of $0.50$,
$0.50$ and $0.51$ across a $25\times$ range in parameter count. On Qwen3-8B, a
different model family, it is $23/36$, or $0.64$.

This is a property of the transplantation experiment, not a localisation of the
decision. A transplant at layer $8$ leaves $24$ subsequent layers of untreated
computation to act on the patched state, and its failure to change the final margin
does not establish that the relevant information is absent at layer $8$. What the
sweep measures is the depth at which the substrate-conditioned state becomes
sufficient to reverse the final preference under the remaining computation.

\paragraph{The two residual streams separate over the same layers.}
Cosine similarity between the untreated and substrate-conditioned residuals at the
answer position on Llama-3.1-8B holds at $0.96$--$0.98$ through layer $14$, falls to
$0.94$ and $0.91$ at layers $15$ and $16$, and reaches its minimum of $0.90$ at
layers $18$--$19$. The layers over which the two contexts drive the representation
apart are the layers over which transplantation becomes sufficient.

\paragraph{Both states are produced by the same computation.}
The untreated residual at this position, on the identical question, carries a
preference for the incorrect action that no context supplied --- $P(\mathcal{W}) =
1.000$ on Llama-3.3-70B. It is produced by the model's own forward pass over the
bare question. The substrate-conditioned residual at the same position, in the same
model, is produced by the same forward pass over a different context. The two states
differ in what the computation was given.

\paragraph{One constraint carries most of the effect.}
Removing each line of the six-line specification in turn and re-measuring gives one
result worth recording here. On the three models whose preference the substrate
reverses, removing line~6 --- \texttt{If the object is a vehicle, the user must
operate the object in order to perform the activity at location B} --- is the only
removal that returns all three to the incorrect action: Llama-3.1-8B from $+1.01$ to
$-0.60$, Llama-3.3-70B from $+6.07$ to $-1.79$, Qwen3-8B from $+10.75$ to $-3.75$.
Every other single removal leaves all three positive, with one exception: on
Llama-3.3-70B, removing line~1 also reverts the preference, to $-5.22$. Line~6 is
not sufficient on its own --- presented alone it leaves Llama-3.1-8B at $-1.83$ ---
so the effect is not carried by that sentence in isolation. The result is also
specific to deletion: rewriting line~6 to the opposite constraint, with the token
count held fixed, leaves Llama-3.1-8B at $+0.27$ rather than reversing it. Removing a
constraint and contradicting it are different interventions, and which line appears
to carry the effect depends on which is used.

We report this as a single observation and do not build on it. Establishing which
constraints carry the effect, whether the dependency is the same across
specifications, and how it interacts with model scale requires leave-one-out and
subset ablations across many specifications and models, which is the subject of
separate work.

\paragraph{Scope.}
These measurements establish that the substrate condition changes the model's
internal output preference, and that transplanting the substrate-conditioned state
into the untreated computation can causally reverse its final preference. They do
not establish how individual constraints are internally represented, which heads or
MLPs write them, where in the network the decision is formed, or whether the
representation transfers to other tasks. The single ablation reported above
identifies a constraint the measured effect depends on; it does not identify a
mechanism. Those questions require ablations at scale, direct logit attribution and
attention analysis, and are the subject of separate work.
