% ============================================================
\section{Internal Causal Measurement}
\label{sec:internal-experiments}
% ============================================================

This section reports the second stage of the study on the ten open-weight models of
the fleet. The measurements establish that the behavioural transition of
Section~\ref{sec:results} corresponds to a change in the computation, and that the
state carrying it can be transplanted. They are not a mechanistic account, and none
is claimed.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:internal-setup}

\paragraph{Cohort and provenance.}
Ten instruction-tuned open-weight models spanning $0.5$B to $70$B parameters and $24$
to $80$ layers are run in \texttt{bf16} with each model's own chat template. The
internal measurements were run on an earlier substrate specification
(\texttt{sha256:6f89be2b}) than the behavioural sweep (\texttt{sha256:5b56feb3}). The
untreated task prompt is identical across both experiments
(\texttt{sha256:f9ac23fb}), as are the conventional intervention prompts. This
section therefore reports a related but distinct substrate condition, and its
margins should not be read as internal measurements of the specification responsible
for the behavioural result of Section~\ref{sec:results}. Both specifications are in
the repository.

\paragraph{Decision margin.}
For each prompt we take a single forward pass and read the decision margin $M$ of
Equation~(\ref{eq:decision-margin}) at the first answer-token prediction position.
$M > 0$ means the \textsc{drive} family carries more total probability than the
\textsc{walk} family. It does not guarantee that the model emits a \textsc{drive}
token: across the margin matrix of Tables~\ref{tab:internal-margins}
and~\ref{tab:conventional-margins}, the sign of $M$ and the greedy token disagree in
three of ninety cells. The margin is supplementary evidence about output preference,
not a substitute for the behavioural classification of Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\paragraph{Transplantation.}
We use activation transplantation to test whether internal states induced by the
substrate can causally change the final action preference. The technique is the
standard causal-mediation instrument for language models
\citep{vig2020causal,meng2022locating}, read out as a difference between two
candidate answer tokens \citep{wang2023interpretability}; we follow the
methodological recommendations of \citet{zhang2024towards}. For each model we first
measure the margin under the untreated task and under the substrate-conditioned
task. We then capture the substrate-conditioned residual stream at the first
answer-token prediction position after each layer $\ell$ and, in models with a
negative untreated margin, replace the corresponding vector in the untreated
computation with it. All other positions are unchanged and the remaining layers of
the untreated computation run normally. We repeat the intervention at each layer and
record the resulting final margin.

Two departures from the usual protocol follow from the question being asked. Causal
tracing normally contrasts a clean prompt with a minimally corrupted one, so that a
restored answer localises to the damaged position; our two conditions differ by the
whole specification, so no positional attribution follows. Consequently we sweep
depth at the answer position rather than the layer $\times$ position grid: the
experiment resolves depth, not sequence position. A position-resolved variant using
token-aligned single-line counterfactuals was run on a subset of these models; we
draw only its controls and one comparison here, and reserve its maps for separate
work.

\paragraph{Inclusion.}
Only model--conditions in which the specification changes the emitted action enter
the causal analysis, following \S\ref{sec:experimental-design}. Models whose margin
remains on the incorrect side under the specification are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:internal-margins} and excluded from it.

\paragraph{Controls.}
Patching a run into itself changes the margin by exactly zero at every layer.
Replacing the transplanted vector with a random direction of the same norm as the
difference between conditions moves the margin by $1.8$ nats on Llama-3.3-70B and
$2.75$ on Qwen3-8B, against effects of $9.5$ and $7.75$ in the same runs; on
Llama-3.1-8B and Llama-3.2-3B it reaches $0.50$ and $0.34$ against effects of $0.74$
and $-0.48$, so those two models are at their own noise floor at line resolution and
no line-level conclusion is drawn from them. A control question whose correct action
is \texttt{walk} remains \texttt{walk} under every patch on every model.

\paragraph{The incorrect action can receive nearly all next-token probability mass.}
Six of the ten models favour the incorrect action on the untreated task. On four of
them the \textsc{walk} token family receives nearly all next-token probability mass:
$P(\mathcal{W})$ rounds to $1.000$ on Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen3-4B-2507 and
Qwen3-8B. It receives $0.975$ on Llama-3.1-8B. Llama-3.2-3B exhibits a less
concentrated preference, with $P(\mathcal{W})=0.606$ and $P(\mathcal{D})=0.375$. In
five of the six models favouring the incorrect action, the first-token distribution
assigns very little probability mass to the correct action.

\paragraph{Conventional interventions move the margin, often away from the correct action.}
Across ten models and seven conventional conditions
(Table~\ref{tab:conventional-margins}), $27$ of $70$ leave the margin further from
the correct action than the untreated task does. On Llama-3.2-3B all seven do. On
Llama-3.1-8B, error avoidance, encouragement and threat take a model at $-3.69$ to
$-4.74$, $-5.87$ and $-5.74$; the behavioural sweep records these conditions as
unchanged, because the emitted word is unchanged. Of the six models favouring the
incorrect action untreated, one conventional condition on one model produces a
positive margin: objective emphasis on Qwen3-4B-2507, at $+6.25$. On the remaining
five the best conventional margin is $1.00$ to $5.25$ nats short of zero.

\paragraph{The specification does not name the action it produces.}
Neither specification contains the word \texttt{drive}. Both name \emph{walking} ---
the incorrect action --- as the operation that fails to satisfy the objective. Both
actions appear in the question itself, identically in every condition. The
specification determines the answer, as any constraint set is meant to; it does not
supply it.

\paragraph{Transplantation reverses the emitted action on three models.}
On Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.3-70B and Qwen3-8B --- the three models where the
specification changes the emitted action --- transplanting the
specification-conditioned residual at a sufficiently deep layer moves the final
margin from $-3.69$, $-13.78$ and $-14.40$ to $+1.01$, $+6.07$ and $+10.75$, in each
case equal to the specification prompt's own margin to two decimals. On Qwen2.5-7B
and Qwen3-4B-2507 the specification does not change the emitted action, and no
transplantation layer does either. Llama-3.2-3B is likewise excluded: the
specification leaves it at $-0.01$, so there is no behavioural transition to study,
and its transplantation result of $+0.47$ is a change in a patched computation with
no corresponding prompt-level state.

\paragraph{The layer at which transplantation becomes sufficient is consistent across scale.}
Transplanting at early layers leaves the final margin unchanged. On Llama-3.1-8B the
patched margin sits at $\approx-3.58$ for layers $0$ through $12$, then moves through
$-3.07$, $-1.72$ and $-0.11$ at layers $13$ to $15$, becomes positive at layer $16$,
and does not increase further. The first positive layer is $16/32$ on Llama-3.1-8B
and $41/80$ on Llama-3.3-70B --- relative depths of $0.50$ and $0.51$ across a
$9\times$ range in parameter count --- and $23/36$, or $0.64$, on Qwen3-8B. This is a
property of the transplantation experiment and not a localisation: a patch at layer
$8$ leaves $24$ subsequent layers of untreated computation to act on the state, and
its failure to change the final margin does not establish that the relevant
information is absent there. What the sweep measures is the depth at which the
specification-conditioned state becomes sufficient to reverse the final preference
under the remaining computation. Over the same layers, cosine similarity between the
untreated and specification-conditioned residuals at this position on Llama-3.1-8B
falls from $0.96$--$0.98$ through layer $14$ to $0.91$ at layer $16$, reaching its
minimum of $0.90$ at layers $18$--$19$.

\paragraph{Behavioural agreement does not imply that the same content carries the decision.}
The position-resolved runs replace one line of the specification at a time with a
rewrite of identical token count that neutralises or reverses its semantics. Two
results follow. Reversing the objective line --- from \texttt{while transporting the
object from location A to B} to \texttt{without transporting the object from location
A to B} --- does not weaken the correct preference on Llama-3.3-70B; it strengthens
it, from $+6.07$ to $+9.04$. And the constraint that carries the effect is not a
property of the specification: reversing line~5 moves Llama-3.3-70B by $9.51$ nats
and reverses its decision, while on Qwen3-8B the same rewrite of the same line moves
the margin below the measurement resolution of that run. Both models reach the same
reproducible action from the same specification. Behavioural convergence therefore
does not entail that the same specification content is doing the work inside them,
and neither the direction of a constraint's contribution nor its identity can be read
off the text.

\paragraph{Scope.}
These measurements establish that the specification changes the models' internal
output preference, and that transplanting the specification-conditioned state into
the untreated computation can reverse the emitted action. They do not establish how
individual constraints are represented, which components write them, where in the
network any decision is formed, or whether the effect transfers to other tasks.
Deletion and reversal disagree --- deleting line~6 reverses Llama-3.1-8B's decision
while rewriting it to the opposite does not --- so which line appears to carry the
effect depends on which intervention is used. Individual constraint ablations, direct
logit attribution, and attention analyses are outside the scope of this paper.
