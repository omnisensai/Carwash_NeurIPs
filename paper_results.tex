\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Behavioural Results}
\label{sec:behavioural-results}

The behavioural fleet is 24 distinct models measured under two protocols. Seventeen
were sampled through hosted APIs; nine open-weight models were sampled locally in
bf16 under a single protocol, with per-sample seeds recorded. Two models
(Llama-3.1-8B, Llama-3.2-3B) appear in both and are counted once, taking the local
row, which is free of the provider-routing term discussed below. All models see
byte-identical prompt text, $T = 1.0$, $\mathrm{top}_p = 1.0$ and $R = 10$ samples
per condition, giving 240 executions per condition.

\paragraph{Untreated operational state.}
Under the untreated question, one of 24 models is reproducibly correct. Sixteen are
reproducibly incorrect, naming the wrong action on all ten samples; seven are
non-reproducible, returning both actions across repetitions of one unchanged input.
The modal untreated state is therefore not error but \emph{consistent} error: for
two thirds of the fleet the failure is invisible to any procedure that samples the
same input twice and compares.

Aggregate accuracy obscures this. The untreated correct-action rate is 18.3\%
(44/240) over the full fleet and 7.6\% (13/170) over the seventeen hosted models;
the difference is carried by four sub-4B open-weight models that answer correctly
without any intervention, and which we show in Section~\ref{sec:internal-results}
answer correctly for the wrong reason. The rate moves by ten points while the
operational state of every individual model is unchanged.

\paragraph{Effects of conventional prompt interventions.}
Seven conventional interventions were applied to the same question under the same
protocol: expert role assignment, objective emphasis, chain-of-thought elicitation,
anti-hallucination instruction, threat, encouragement, and error-avoidance
instruction. None changes the operational state of the fleet
(Table~\ref{tab:conditions}). The strongest, expert role assignment, raises the
correct-action rate from 18.3\% to 39.2\% while converting exactly one of the
sixteen reproducibly incorrect models to reproducible correctness. No conventional
condition converts more than one. Six of the seven leave at least fourteen of the
sixteen reproducibly incorrect.

The pattern is sharper on the open-weight fleet, where four models are reproducibly
incorrect untreated: across seven conditions and those four models --- 28
model-condition cells --- 27 are 0/10 and one is 4/10. No conventional intervention
produces a single reproducibly correct cell among them.

\paragraph{Specification-conditioned operational state.}
Under the task specification, supplied as a system message with the question
unchanged, 203 of 240 executions name the correct action (84.6\%; 95.9\% over the
seventeen hosted models). Eighteen of 24 models are reproducibly correct and two
remain reproducibly incorrect --- Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B-2507, whose internal
measurements in Section~\ref{sec:internal-results} show large but insufficient
displacement rather than no effect.

\paragraph{Transitions between reproducibly incorrect, non-reproducible, and
reproducibly correct states.}
The quantity of interest is not the aggregate rate but the transition each model
makes from its untreated state. Restricting attention to the sixteen models that are
reproducibly incorrect untreated (Table~\ref{tab:transitions}), the task
specification moves thirteen to reproducible correctness and one to
non-reproducibility. Every conventional intervention leaves at least twelve of the
sixteen unchanged, and three of the seven move none of them to reproducible
correctness at all.

This separation is categorical rather than one of degree, and
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} shows why the aggregate rate cannot express it. Under
encouragement the fleet rate falls below untreated while the reproducibly incorrect
count \emph{rises} by one; under chain of thought the rate rises by 1.5 points with
the same count. Conventional interventions largely move models within and between
the non-reproducible and reproducibly incorrect bands --- between consistent and
inconsistent error --- which changes the observed rate without changing whether any
given model can be relied on for the decision.

\paragraph{Directionality controls.}
Three observations bear on whether the specification acts as a task description or
merely as a bias toward one answer token. They constrain direction, not scope: we do
not test whether a specification written for one task transfers to another, and no
claim of generalisation is made here.

First, the effect is not unidirectional. Qwen2.5-0.5B answers correctly on 7 of 10
untreated samples and on 3 of 10 under the specification, moving away from the
correct action; its decision margin moves correspondingly, from $+0.75$ to $-0.47$
nats. A generic bias toward the correct token would not produce this.

Second, the specification does not disturb a model that is already reproducibly
correct: Qwen2.5-3B answers correctly on 10 of 10 samples untreated and under the
specification.

Third, the two models measured under both protocols give a consistency check, and
one that does not fully resolve at $R=10$. Llama-3.1-8B is reproducibly incorrect
untreated under both protocols; under the specification it answers correctly on 7 of
10 samples through the routed API and 10 of 10 locally, which would place it in
different reproducibility classes depending on the route. Llama-3.2-3B is
non-reproducible under both, at 8/10 and 4/10. Neither difference is significant at
this sample size (Fisher two-sided $p = 0.21$ and $p = 0.17$), so we report them as
unresolved rather than as a measured effect of the serving stack; separating the two
would need roughly $R = 15$ per arm.

What the per-sample records do establish is that the hosted rows carry a term the
local rows do not. The ten API samples for Llama-3.1-8B under the specification were
served by three different upstream providers within the single condition, and the
incorrect samples are split across two of them (Novita 4/6 correct, DeepInfra 2/3,
Cloudflare 1/1), so no one backend accounts for them. Within-condition spread on
these rows is therefore not sampling variance alone --- it also contains whatever
differs between backends in precision, kernels and serving stack --- and it cannot
be decomposed after the fact. We take the local rows as the model-level measurement
for that reason, not because the two are shown to differ.

\subsection{Internal Causal Results}
\label{sec:internal-results}

\paragraph{Untreated and specification-conditioned decision preference.}
On ten open-weight models we measure the decision margin
$M = \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{D}} P(t) - \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{W}} P(t)$
at the position that predicts the first answer token, teacher-forced, under the
chat template, in bf16 (Table~\ref{tab:margins}). Six models prefer the
incorrect action untreated, with margins from $-0.50$ to $-22.00$ nats. Under
the specification, four of the six cross zero.

The conventional interventions do not. On Llama-3.3-70B, whose untreated margin
is $-13.65$, the seven conventional conditions span $-18.21$ to $-4.67$ nats:
every one remains on the incorrect side, and one (chain of thought) moves the
margin further from the correct action than no intervention at all. The
specification reaches $+15.01$. On Llama-3.1-8B the pattern repeats at smaller
scale: conventional conditions span $-4.57$ to $-0.35$ against an untreated
$-3.71$, and the specification reaches $+1.51$. Across both models, no
conventional intervention crosses zero.

\paragraph{Activation transplantation.}
To test whether the specification's effect is carried by the residual stream at
the decision position rather than by the presence of its tokens in context, we
run the untreated prompt and replace the residual at the answer position, at a
single layer, with the residual from the specification run at the same position
and layer. No specification token is present in the patched forward pass. In
every model whose preference the specification reverses, this transplant alone
reverses the untreated preference. The specification's effect is therefore
present as a state at one position, and is transferable without the text that
produced it.

Two controls bound the procedure. Patching a run with its own residual
(same-run $S \rightarrow S$) produces $\Delta M = 0.00$ in all 96 cells per
model, confirming the intervention machinery contributes nothing on its own.
Patching a random direction of matched norm produces $\max |\Delta M|$ of
$0.87$, $0.39$ and $2.00$ nats on Llama-3.3-70B, Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B,
against measured single-line effects reaching $2.29$, $1.99$ and $14.75$ nats
in the same models. The effect is not a norm perturbation.

\paragraph{Layerwise causal effects.}
The transplant is effective from the middle of the stack: layer 13 of 28, 15 of
32, 38 of 80 and 24 of 36, that is between $0.46$ and $0.67$ of depth. Below
those layers the patched margin is indistinguishable from untreated; above
them, the patched run answers correctly. The decision is thus determined well
before the layers at which the unembedding makes it visible --- the logit lens
resolves the reversal only in the last few layers, underestimating by 10 to 40
layers the depth at which the constraint is installed.

\paragraph{Successful and unsuccessful preference reversals.}
Four of the six models that prefer the incorrect action untreated cross zero
under the specification; two do not. Both failures move substantially in the
correct direction without arriving: Qwen2.5-7B from $-10.50$ to $-1.50$
(9.0 nats) and Qwen3-4B-2507 from $-22.00$ to $-5.50$ (16.5 nats). Their
behavioural rows agree: both answer incorrectly on 10 of 10 samples under the
specification.

These are not cases in which the specification fails to act. They are cases in
which it acts and the displacement is insufficient. The distinction matters for
what the method claims: a specification that reverses a preference in four
models of six, and moves the margin by 9 to 16 nats in the two it does not
reverse, is an intervention of graded strength, not a switch.

\paragraph{Relationship between internal and behavioural measurements.}
The two measurements are taken on the same models and the same prompt text but
by different procedures --- one deterministic forward pass against ten sampled
generations --- and they agree quantitatively. Treating
$\sigma(M)$ as a predicted correct-action rate, predicted and observed counts
agree within 1.5 of 10 samples in 14 of 18 model-condition cells, with a
maximum discrepancy of 2.2 samples. In no cell do the two disagree in sign:
every model's majority sampled action matches the sign of its margin.

The four larger discrepancies are all in the direction of greater determinism
than the margin predicts: models at intermediate margins emit the favoured
action more consistently than a first-token reading implies. We note this as an
observation about the relationship between the two readouts rather than a
correction to either, since the margin is measured at a single teacher-forced
position while sampling runs the full generation.
