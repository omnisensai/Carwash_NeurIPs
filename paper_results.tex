\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Behavioural Results}
\label{sec:behavioural-results}

The question is whether a model that fails this decision can be moved to
reproducible correctness. We measured 23 models that fail it at baseline, either
confidently wrong or unreliable: each names the wrong action on all ten baseline
samples, or names different actions across them. Fifteen are sampled
through hosted APIs and eight are open-weight models sampled locally in bf16 with a
per-sample seed recorded. Every model receives byte-identical prompt text, $R=10$
samples per condition, giving 230 executions per condition. The baseline prompt,
the seven conventional prompts and the substrate are reproduced verbatim in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}, each with the sha256 digest recorded with every
execution.

\paragraph{Baseline operational state.}
Sixteen of the 23 models are reproducibly incorrect, naming the wrong action on all
ten samples. Seven are non-reproducible. Thirty-four of 230 executions name the
intended action.

\paragraph{Effects of conventional prompt interventions.}
Seven conventional interventions were applied to the same question under the same
protocol: expert role assignment, objective emphasis, chain-of-thought elicitation,
anti-hallucination instruction, threat, encouragement, and error-avoidance
instruction. Each adds a single line to the baseline prompt, inserted between the
question and the answer instruction, except expert role assignment, which precedes
the question; the texts are reproduced verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.
Table~\ref{tab:conditions} gives the operational state of the 23 models under every
condition, abbreviated RC for reproducibly correct, NR for non-reproducible and RI
for reproducibly incorrect. Expert role assignment converts one of the
sixteen reproducibly incorrect models to reproducible correctness; no conventional
condition converts more than one, and across all seven the converted model is the
same one. Four of the seven leave at least fifteen of the sixteen reproducibly
incorrect. Five of the seven move a non-reproducible model to reproducible
incorrectness: one each under anti-hallucination, chain-of-thought, error avoidance
and threat, and two under encouragement.

\paragraph{Substrate-conditioned operational state.}
Under the substrate, supplied as a system message with the question unchanged,
seventeen of the 23 models are reproducibly correct, four are non-reproducible, and
two are reproducibly incorrect (Qwen2.5-7B and Qwen3-4B-2507);
Table~\ref{tab:per-model} gives every model's count at both conditions. One hundred and
ninety-three of 230 executions name the intended action. No model moves to
reproducible incorrectness. One model names the intended action less often than at
baseline (Qwen2.5-0.5B, 7/10 to 3/10) without leaving the non-reproducible state,
and one is unchanged sample for sample (Llama 3.2-3B, 4/10 at both conditions, the
same four sample indices).

\paragraph{Transitions between reproducibly incorrect, non-reproducible, and
reproducibly correct states.}
Transitions are reported separately for the two baseline failure modes
(Table~\ref{tab:transitions}). The substrate moves thirteen of the sixteen
reproducibly incorrect models and four of the seven non-reproducible models to
reproducible correctness, seventeen of 23 in total, in a single pass with the
question unchanged and no sampling, voting or retry. Every conventional intervention
reaches reproducible correctness in at most one model from either group.

\subsection{Internal Causal Results}
\label{sec:internal-results}

We measured internal quantities on nine of the 23 models, running their weights
locally, on prompt text byte-identical to the behavioural runs. Eight are the
locally sampled models of Table~\ref{tab:per-model}; Llama 3.3-70B is sampled
behaviourally through a hosted API and run locally for this measurement. The
readout is the decision margin
\begin{equation}
M \;=\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{D}} P(t) \;-\; \log \sum_{t \in \mathcal{W}} P(t)
\end{equation}
in nats, at the position that predicts the first answer token, teacher-forced, under
the chat template, in bf16; $M > 0$ favours the intended action.

\paragraph{Baseline and substrate-conditioned decision preference.}
Six of the nine prefer the incorrect action at baseline, with margins from $-0.50$
to $-22.00$ nats (Table~\ref{tab:margins}). The remaining three are
non-reproducible behaviourally but favour the intended action at the answer position
and so cannot exhibit a margin reversal. Under the substrate, four of the six cross
zero.

On Llama 3.3-70B, whose baseline margin is $-13.65$, the seven conventional
conditions of Table~\ref{tab:conditions} span $-18.21$ to $-5.51$ nats; the
substrate reaches $+15.01$. On Llama 3.1-8B they span $-4.57$ to $-0.83$ against a
baseline of $-3.71$; the substrate reaches $+1.51$. No conventional intervention
crosses zero in either model.

\paragraph{Activation transplantation.}
We run the baseline prompt and replace the residual stream at the answer position,
at a single layer, with the residual from the substrate run at the same position and
layer. No substrate token is present in the patched forward pass. In each of the
four models whose preference the substrate reverses, this transplant alone reverses
the baseline preference.

Three controls were run. Patching a run with its own residual gives $\Delta M =
0.00$ in all 96 cells per model. Patching a random direction of matched norm gives a
maximum $|\Delta M|$ of $0.87$, $0.39$ and $2.00$ nats on Llama 3.3-70B,
Llama 3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B, against measured single-line effects of $2.29$, $1.99$
and $14.75$ nats in the same models. Removing every constraint line and retaining
only the substrate's section headers gives $-4.07$ against a baseline of $-3.71$ on
Llama 3.1-8B, $-14.92$ against $-13.65$ on Llama 3.3-70B, and $-13.75$ against
$-14.06$ on Qwen3-8B.

\paragraph{Layerwise causal effects.}
The transplant is effective from layer 13 of 28, 15 of 32, 38 of 80 and 24 of 36,
that is between $0.48$ and $0.69$ of depth. Below those layers the patched margin is
within noise of the baseline value; above them the patched run selects the intended
action.

The three Llama models cross at $0.48$ of depth in each case, across a
$2.9\times$ range in layer count, and as a step: the patched margin is flat at the
baseline value and then moves to its plateau within one tenth of depth. Qwen3-8B
crosses at $0.69$ and climbs across the final third. It is the only Qwen in the set
that crosses.

\paragraph{Successful and unsuccessful preference reversals.}
Four of the six models that prefer the incorrect action at baseline cross zero under
the substrate; two do not. Qwen2.5-7B moves from $-10.50$ to $-1.50$, a displacement
of $9.0$ nats, and Qwen3-4B-2507 from $-22.00$ to $-5.50$, a displacement of $16.5$
nats. Their layerwise curves rise monotonically across the back half of the stack
and terminate short of zero at the final layer. Both emit the incorrect action on
all ten samples under the substrate.

\paragraph{Relationship between internal and behavioural measurements.}
Treating $\sigma(M)$ as a predicted count of intended actions in ten samples,
predicted and observed counts agree within $1.5$ samples in 14 of the 18
model--condition cells measured both ways, with a maximum discrepancy of $2.2$
samples. In no cell do the two disagree in sign. The four larger discrepancies are
all in the direction of greater determinism than the margin predicts.
