\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ============================================================

We measured one binary operational decision across $28$ language models, ten input
conditions and ten single-pass executions per pair: $2{,}800$ executions in which
weights, decoding configuration and retry policy are fixed and only the text around
an unchanged question varies. At baseline no model is reproducibly correct; $21$ are
reproducibly incorrect and seven are non-reproducible. Seven conventional prompt
interventions, applied through the same channel, convert one reproducibly incorrect
model between them, and five of the seven convert at least one non-reproducible model
into a reproducibly incorrect one --- making its failure both worse and harder to
detect. One shared substrate, written once and applied unchanged to every model,
converts $18$ of the $21$ and reaches reproducible correctness on $22$ of the $28$.

We do not offer that text as a remedy. It is held fixed so that something else can
be read off: with the input constant across every model, what varies is the model.

The substrate never names the correct action. It states the object, the service
location, the objective, and the relations that make the incorrect action fail to
achieve it. The condition that does name the correct action is not a stronger version
of this: it leaves seven models reproducibly incorrect, on all of which the substrate
is reproducibly correct. Specifying the task and asserting the answer are different
interventions with different results on the same models.

In the $14$ open-weight models we can measure inside, the behavioural transition has
an internal counterpart. Transplanting the substrate-conditioned residual at the
answer position, after a single layer, into an otherwise unmodified baseline
computation reverses the decision margin in each of the nine models whose preference
the substrate reverses, with no substrate token present in the patched context. The
transition is localised: the margin sits flat at its baseline value through most of
the network and then crosses within a narrow band of layers. Patching a run with its
own residual moves the margin by $0.0000$ nats at every layer of every model; the
substrate's scaffolding with its constraint lines removed crosses zero on none of
those nine.

Read as an instrument rather than a remedy, the fixed specification measures the
population it is applied to. The same $585$ characters move the decision margin by
$-0.93$ nats on Qwen2.5-0.5B and by $+31.75$ on Qwen2.5-14B: one text, an identical
question, and $32.68$ nats of spread in how the models answer it. Thirteen of the
$14$ move toward the intended action and one moves away. Behaviourally the same
split appears: $22$ of the $28$ converge on identical output and six do not, and the
six differ from each other in kind --- two stay uniformly wrong on a margin shift
that is large but insufficient, two reach $8/10$, one is unmoved, one is made worse.
Under an underspecified input these models fail in ways that are hard to compare,
because each is failing at a different thing. Under a specification held constant
they become comparable, and the residual divergence is a property of the models.
Varying the prompt per model to maximise each one's score would remove exactly the
contrast that makes this visible.

What these results establish is narrow and we state it as such. Sufficiency is
established for particular model--condition pairs on one task, not for a universal
specification and not for transfer. Six models are not rescued, two of them not at
all, and one moves away from the intended action. The transplant shows that a state
is sufficient to reverse the measured preference; it does not identify the mechanism
that produces the preference, and the line ablations show that no single constraint
line is necessary across the population nor sufficient across it. A reversed margin
is not reproducibly correct behaviour, which is established by sampling rather than
by a forward pass.

The contribution is therefore a criterion and a control variable, not a prompt. The
model--condition pair, tested against a requirement that admits no partial credit, is
a unit that carries a reliability claim where an aggregate score does not; and a
specification that can be written once, hashed, and applied unchanged across models
is an experimental control, which is what makes a verified behavioural transition ---
reproducibly incorrect under one declared input, reproducibly correct under another
--- a stable object for internal analysis where a single successful generation is
not.

Nothing here suggests that a sufficient specification makes the choice of model
unimportant. The measurements point the other way: holding the input fixed is what
exposes how unlike each other these models are on one binary decision, and six of
them are not brought to the declared target state by the specification that suffices
for twenty-two. What such a specification must contain, whether any of this transfers
to another task, why a model rejects an asserted answer it accepts as a stated
relation, and what distinguishes the models that converge under a fixed
specification from those that do not, are open.
